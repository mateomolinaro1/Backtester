### Ex-Ante Portfolio Volatility Without a Factor Model

Without imposing a factor structure on asset returns, the ex-ante volatility
of the Pure Alpha portfolio can be estimated directly from the full forecast
covariance matrix of asset returns.

Let

$$
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\in
\mathbb{R}^{N_t}
$$

denote the Pure Alpha portfolio weights before the isovol step.

Let

$$
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
\in
\mathbb{R}^{N_t \times N_t}
$$

denote the point-in-time forecast covariance matrix of asset returns.

The ex-ante portfolio variance is

$$
\boxed{
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}^{2}
=
\left(\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}\right)^\top
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}.
}
$$

Therefore, the ex-ante portfolio volatility is

$$
\boxed{
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}
=
\sqrt{
\left(\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}\right)^\top
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
}.
}
$$

### Expanded Form

Writing the covariance matrix explicitly, portfolio variance can equivalently
be expressed as

$$
\boxed{
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}^{2}
=
\sum_{i=1}^{N_t}
\left(w_{i,t}^{PA}\right)^2
\widehat{\sigma}_{i,t}^{2}
+
2
\sum_{i<j}
w_{i,t}^{PA}
w_{j,t}^{PA}
\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}_t(R_i,R_j).
}
$$

The first term captures the contribution of individual asset variances, while
the second captures all pairwise covariance contributions.

Using

$$
\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}_t(R_i,R_j)
=
\widehat{\rho}_{ij,t}
\widehat{\sigma}_{i,t}
\widehat{\sigma}_{j,t},
$$

the same expression can be written as

$$
\boxed{
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}^{2}
=
\sum_i
\left(w_{i,t}^{PA}\right)^2
\widehat{\sigma}_{i,t}^{2}
+
2
\sum_{i<j}
w_{i,t}^{PA}
w_{j,t}^{PA}
\widehat{\rho}_{ij,t}
\widehat{\sigma}_{i,t}
\widehat{\sigma}_{j,t}.
}
$$

### Isovol Scaling

Let

$$
\sigma^*
$$

denote the desired portfolio volatility target, expressed at the same
frequency as the covariance estimate.

The unconstrained volatility-scaling multiplier is

$$
\boxed{
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{vol}}
=
\frac{
\sigma^*
}{
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}
}.
}
$$

The risk-scaled Pure Alpha weights are then

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}
=
\lambda_t
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA},
}
$$

where $\lambda_t$ may additionally incorporate leverage or other risk-scaling
caps.

For example, if $\lambda_{\max}$ represents a maximum scaling multiplier,

$$
\boxed{
\lambda_t
=
\min
\left(
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{vol}},
\lambda_{\max}
\right).
}
$$

If instead the constraint is expressed as a maximum final gross exposure
$G_{\max}$, then

$$
\boxed{
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{gross}}
=
\frac{
G_{\max}
}{
\left\|\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}\right\|_1
},
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\lambda_t
=
\min
\left(
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{vol}},
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{gross}}
\right).
}
$$

### Generic Risk-Model Interface

The generic portfolio-risk calculation should therefore be based on

$$
\boxed{
\widehat{\sigma}_{p,t}
=
\sqrt{
\mathbf{w}_t^\top
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
\mathbf{w}_t
}.
}
$$

This should be the general interface used by the backtester.

The method used to construct

$$
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
$$

is a separate concern. For example, it may be obtained from:

- a historical sample covariance estimator;
- an exponentially weighted covariance estimator;
- a shrinkage estimator;
- a factor risk model;
- another covariance forecasting model.

Thus, the portfolio volatility estimator should consume a point-in-time
covariance matrix without requiring knowledge of how that covariance matrix
was estimated.

This separation keeps the portfolio risk and isovol components independent
from the particular risk-model implementation.
