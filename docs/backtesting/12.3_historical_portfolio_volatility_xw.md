# Historical Portfolio Volatility via the $Xw$ Computation

## Objective

For the V1 backtester, the portfolio volatility estimator is currently consumed only by the isovol step.

Because isovol only requires the scalar ex-ante portfolio volatility

$$
\widehat{\sigma}_{p,t},
$$

it is not necessary to explicitly construct the full $N_t \times N_t$ covariance matrix when using a simple historical sample covariance estimator.

Instead, the same quantity can be obtained more efficiently by applying the **current portfolio weights** to the historical return panel and computing the standard deviation of the resulting hypothetical portfolio return series.

The V1 estimator is therefore based on

$$
\boxed{
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}
=
\operatorname{Std}
\left(
\mathbf{X}_t \mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\right)
}.
$$

---

## 1. Current Portfolio Weights

At rebalance date $t$, let

$$
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\in
\mathbb{R}^{N_t}
$$

denote the current Pure Alpha portfolio weights **before the isovol step**, where $N_t$ is the number of securities in the portfolio-risk universe at date $t$.

These are the weights whose ex-ante risk must be estimated.

Importantly, the vector $\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}$ is held fixed throughout the historical risk-estimation window.

---

## 2. Historical Return Window

Let $L$ denote the historical lookback length.

For each historical date

$$
\tau = t-L,\ldots,t-1,
$$

let

$$
\mathbf{R}_{\tau}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
R_{1,\tau} \\
R_{2,\tau} \\
\vdots \\
R_{N_t,\tau}
\end{bmatrix}
\in
\mathbb{R}^{N_t}
$$

denote the vector of asset returns.

Stack these historical return vectors into the matrix

$$
\mathbf{X}_t
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{R}_{t-L}^{\top} \\
\mathbf{R}_{t-L+1}^{\top} \\
\vdots \\
\mathbf{R}_{t-1}^{\top}
\end{bmatrix}
\in
\mathbb{R}^{L \times N_t}.
$$

Therefore:

- each row corresponds to one historical date;
- each column corresponds to one security;
- $\mathbf{X}_t$ contains only information available before the risk-scaling decision at date $t$.

---

## 3. Historical Hypothetical Returns of the Current Portfolio

Apply the **current** portfolio weights retrospectively to every historical return vector.

For each $\tau \in \{t-L,\ldots,t-1\}$,

$$
\widetilde{R}_{PA,\tau}^{(t)}
=
\left(
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\right)^{\top}
\mathbf{R}_{\tau}.
$$

The superscript $(t)$ is important.

These quantities are **not** the realized historical returns of the strategy.

They answer the counterfactual question:

> What return would today's portfolio composition have generated if it had been exposed to the historical asset returns observed at date $\tau$?

Stacking these hypothetical returns gives

$$
\widetilde{\mathbf{r}}_{PA,t}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\widetilde{R}_{PA,t-L}^{(t)} \\
\widetilde{R}_{PA,t-L+1}^{(t)} \\
\vdots \\
\widetilde{R}_{PA,t-1}^{(t)}
\end{bmatrix}
\in
\mathbb{R}^{L}.
$$

In matrix notation,

$$
\boxed{
\widetilde{\mathbf{r}}_{PA,t}
=
\mathbf{X}_t
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
}.
$$

The dimensions are

$$
\underbrace{\mathbf{X}_t}_{L \times N_t}
\underbrace{\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}}_{N_t \times 1}
=
\underbrace{\widetilde{\mathbf{r}}_{PA,t}}_{L \times 1}.
$$

---

## 4. Historical Ex-Ante Portfolio Volatility

The historical ex-ante portfolio variance is estimated as the sample variance of the hypothetical return series

$$
\widetilde{\mathbf{r}}_{PA,t}
=
\mathbf{X}_t \mathbf{w}_t^{PA}.
$$

Define its sample mean as

$$
\overline{\widetilde{R}}_{PA,t}
=
\frac{1}{L}
\sum_{\tau=t-L}^{t-1}
\widetilde{R}_{PA,\tau}^{(t)}.
$$

The sample variance estimator is

$$
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}^{\,2}
=
\frac{1}{L-1}
\sum_{\tau=t-L}^{t-1}
\left(
\widetilde{R}_{PA,\tau}^{(t)}
-
\overline{\widetilde{R}}_{PA,t}
\right)^2.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}
=
\operatorname{Std}_{\tau=t-L,\ldots,t-1}
\left(
\widetilde{R}_{PA,\tau}^{(t)}
\right)
}.
$$

Using the matrix representation,

$$
\boxed{
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}
=
\operatorname{Std}
\left(
\mathbf{X}_t
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\right)
}.
$$

For the V1 implementation, the sample standard deviation should use

$$
\mathrm{ddof}=1,
$$

so that the denominator is $L-1$.

---

## 5. Equivalence with the Covariance-Matrix Formula

The $Xw$ computation is not a different risk model from the historical sample covariance estimator.

It is an algebraically equivalent and computationally cheaper way of evaluating the same portfolio variance when only the scalar portfolio risk is required.

Let

$$
\overline{\mathbf{R}}_t
=
\frac{1}{L}
\sum_{\tau=t-L}^{t-1}
\mathbf{R}_{\tau}
\in
\mathbb{R}^{N_t}
$$

denote the vector of historical mean returns.

Define the demeaned historical return matrix

$$
\mathbf{X}_t^{c}
=
\mathbf{X}_t
-
\mathbf{1}_L
\overline{\mathbf{R}}_t^{\top},
$$

where

$$
\mathbf{1}_L
\in
\mathbb{R}^{L}
$$

is a vector of ones.

The historical sample covariance matrix is

$$
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
=
\frac{1}{L-1}
\left(
\mathbf{X}_t^{c}
\right)^{\top}
\mathbf{X}_t^{c}.
$$

Its dimensions are

$$
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
\in
\mathbb{R}^{N_t \times N_t}.
$$

The standard portfolio-variance formula is

$$
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}^{\,2}
=
\left(
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\right)^{\top}
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}.
$$

Substituting the sample covariance estimator,

$$
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}^{\,2}
=
\left(
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\right)^{\top}
\left[
\frac{1}{L-1}
\left(
\mathbf{X}_t^{c}
\right)^{\top}
\mathbf{X}_t^{c}
\right]
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}.
$$

Rearranging the scalar expression,

$$
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}^{\,2}
=
\frac{1}{L-1}
\left(
\mathbf{X}_t^{c}
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\right)^{\top}
\left(
\mathbf{X}_t^{c}
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\right).
$$

But

$$
\mathbf{X}_t^{c}
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
$$

is simply the demeaned hypothetical historical return series of today's portfolio.

Hence,

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}
\left(
\mathbf{X}_t
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\right)
=
\left(
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\right)^{\top}
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
}.
$$

Taking square roots,

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Std}
\left(
\mathbf{X}_t
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\right)
=
\sqrt{
\left(
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\right)^{\top}
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
}
}.
$$

This equivalence requires the same:

- historical observations;
- return definitions;
- demeaning convention;
- missing-data policy;
- degrees-of-freedom convention.

---

## 6. Why the $Xw$ Computation Is Preferable for V1

If the only quantity required by the current consumer is

$$
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t},
$$

explicitly constructing the full covariance matrix

$$
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
\in
\mathbb{R}^{N_t \times N_t}
$$

is unnecessary.

The full covariance route is

$$
\mathbf{X}_t
\longrightarrow
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
\longrightarrow
\left(
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\right)^{\top}
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\longrightarrow
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}.
$$

The direct route is

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf{X}_t
\longrightarrow
\mathbf{X}_t \mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Std}
\left(
\mathbf{X}_t \mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\right)
\longrightarrow
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}
}.
$$

For $L$ historical observations and $N_t$ securities:

- constructing a dense covariance matrix requires approximately $O(LN_t^2)$ operations and $O(N_t^2)$ storage;
- computing $\mathbf{X}_t \mathbf{w}_t^{PA}$ requires approximately $O(LN_t)$ operations;
- the resulting hypothetical portfolio-return vector contains only $L$ observations.

For a large equity universe, this can materially reduce both computation time and memory usage.

---

## 7. Why the Weights Are Fixed at Date $t$

The weights used throughout the historical risk-estimation window are

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
},
$$

not the historical portfolio weights

$$
\mathbf{w}_{\tau}^{PA}.
$$

This is intentional.

The purpose of the isovol estimator at date $t$ is to answer:

> Given the portfolio I am about to hold at date $t$, what is my estimate of its volatility?

The current portfolio composition is known:

$$
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}.
$$

Future returns are unknown.

The recent historical joint return distribution is therefore used as the empirical risk environment against which the current portfolio is evaluated.

Conceptually,

$$
\boxed{
\text{Current Portfolio Composition}
+
\text{Historical Joint Returns}
\longrightarrow
\text{Current Ex-Ante Risk Estimate}
}.
$$

Using time-varying historical weights would instead produce

$$
R_{PA,\tau}
=
\left(
\mathbf{w}_{\tau}^{PA}
\right)^{\top}
\mathbf{R}_{\tau},
$$

whose standard deviation measures the historical volatility of the **evolving strategy**.

That is a useful performance statistic, but it answers a different question.

---

## 8. Risk-Estimation Returns vs. Realized Backtest Returns

The implementation must clearly distinguish two different return series.

### 8.1 Historical Hypothetical Return of the Current Portfolio

For risk estimation,

$$
\boxed{
\widetilde{R}_{PA,\tau}^{(t)}
=
\left(
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\right)^{\top}
\mathbf{R}_{\tau}
}.
$$

The weights are fixed at their current date-$t$ values.

This series is used to estimate

$$
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}.
$$

### 8.2 Realized Historical Backtest Return

For backtest accounting,

$$
\boxed{
R_{PA,\tau}^{\mathrm{realized}}
=
\left(
\mathbf{w}_{\tau^-}^{\mathrm{implemented}}
\right)^{\top}
\mathbf{R}_{\tau}
}.
$$

Here,

$$
\mathbf{w}_{\tau^-}^{\mathrm{implemented}}
$$

denotes the portfolio actually held immediately before the return over period $\tau$ is realized.

This series is used for realized performance evaluation.

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{
\text{Risk Estimation Return}
\neq
\text{Realized Backtest Return}
}.
$$

These objects should have separate names, types, or clearly separated responsibilities in the implementation.

---

## 9. Point-in-Time Requirement

The volatility estimator must be strictly point-in-time.

At risk-estimation date $t$, the return matrix $\mathbf{X}_t$ may contain only observations available before the risk-scaling decision.

For a daily strategy where the current portfolio is formed using information available at $t$, a typical historical risk window is

$$
\tau=t-L,\ldots,t-1.
$$

Thus,

$$
\boxed{
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}
=
f
\left(
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA},
\mathbf{R}_{t-L},
\ldots,
\mathbf{R}_{t-1}
\right)
}.
$$

The exact endpoint must remain consistent with the backtest timing convention.

No future return may enter the estimator.

---

## 10. Missing-Return Policy

The $Xw$ formulation avoids explicitly constructing a pairwise covariance matrix, but it does **not** eliminate the missing-data problem.

The historical return panel may contain missing observations because of:

- IPOs;
- delistings;
- trading suspensions;
- market-specific holidays;
- data-quality problems;
- other gaps.

V1 must not silently replace an unavailable return by zero.

In general,

$$
\boxed{
\text{Missing Return}
\neq
0\% \text{ Return}
}.
$$

The implementation must therefore define explicitly:

1. the minimum historical coverage required for a materially weighted security;
2. the treatment of newly listed securities;
3. the treatment of delisted securities;
4. the treatment of isolated missing observations;
5. the minimum number of valid historical portfolio-return observations required to estimate volatility;
6. the behavior when these requirements are not satisfied.

If the available historical sample is insufficient, the estimator should fail explicitly or return a clearly defined unavailable result rather than fabricate a volatility estimate.

This policy should be configurable and tested independently.

---

## 11. Isovol Scaling

Once the historical ex-ante volatility

$$
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}
$$

has been estimated, let

$$
\sigma_{PA}^{*}
$$

denote the desired portfolio volatility target.

The target and estimated volatility must be expressed at the same frequency.

The unconstrained volatility-scaling multiplier is

$$
\boxed{
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{vol}}
=
\frac{
\sigma_{PA}^{*}
}{
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}
}
}.
$$

If $\lambda_{\max}$ denotes a maximum allowed scaling multiplier, define

$$
\boxed{
\lambda_t
=
\min
\left(
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{vol}},
\lambda_{\max}
\right)
}.
$$

The isovol-scaled portfolio is then

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}
=
\lambda_t
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
}.
$$

If the cap is instead defined as a maximum final gross exposure $G_{\max}$, then

$$
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{gross}}
=
\frac{
G_{\max}
}{
\left\|
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\right\|_1
},
$$

and the final multiplier can be defined as

$$
\boxed{
\lambda_t
=
\min
\left(
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{vol}},
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{gross}}
\right)
}.
$$

## 12. Recommended V1 Interface

The V1 component should conceptually implement

```text
current Pure Alpha weights
        +
point-in-time trailing asset returns
        |
        v
historical hypothetical returns of current portfolio
        |
        v
historical ex-ante portfolio volatility
        |
        v
isovol multiplier
        |
        v
isovol-scaled Pure Alpha weights
```

A possible interface is

```python
PortfolioVolatilityEstimator.estimate_at(
    date=t,
    weights=current_pure_alpha_weights,
    returns=historical_returns,
) -> float
```

For the V1 historical implementation, the central numerical computation is equivalent to

```python
portfolio_returns = X @ weights
sigma = portfolio_returns.std(ddof=1)
```

subject to:

- point-in-time slicing;
- security alignment;
- missing-data validation;
- minimum-history requirements;
- numerical validation.

The component does not need to materialize or expose a full covariance matrix.

---

## 13. Required Validation

At each risk-estimation date, the implementation should validate at least the following conditions.

### Dimension Alignment

$$
\mathbf{X}_t
\in
\mathbb{R}^{L \times N_t},
\qquad
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\in
\mathbb{R}^{N_t}.
$$

The security ordering of the columns of $\mathbf{X}_t$ must exactly match the security ordering of $\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}$.

### Finite Weights

All portfolio weights used by the estimator must be finite.

### Historical Coverage

The return observations required by the configured missing-data policy must be available.

### Positive Sample Length

There must be enough valid historical observations to estimate a sample standard deviation.

### Finite Volatility

The resulting estimate must satisfy

$$
0
\leq
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}
<
\infty.
$$

A zero or near-zero volatility estimate requires an explicit policy before computing

$$
\frac{\sigma_{PA}^{*}}{\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}},
$$

because otherwise the scaling multiplier becomes undefined or excessively large.

---

## 14. Future Extension

The $Xw$ shortcut is exact for the simple historical sample-covariance estimator.

It should not be interpreted as a universal replacement for covariance or risk models.

Future implementations may use:

- exponentially weighted covariance estimation;
- Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage;
- other shrinkage estimators;
- factor risk models;
- structured covariance models;
- volatility forecasting models.

For such estimators, the generic portfolio-risk expression remains

$$
\boxed{
\widehat{\sigma}_{p,t}
=
\sqrt{
\mathbf{w}_t^{\top}
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
\mathbf{w}_t
}
}.
$$

The method used to estimate risk should therefore remain abstracted from the isovol component.

For V1, however, when the desired risk model is the ordinary historical sample covariance estimator and the only required output is portfolio volatility, the preferred implementation is

$$
\boxed{
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}
=
\operatorname{Std}
\left(
\mathbf{X}_t
\mathbf{w}_t^{PA}
\right)
}.
$$

This computes exactly the historical sample-covariance portfolio volatility required by the isovol step without unnecessarily constructing an $N_t \times N_t$ covariance matrix.
