# Pure Alpha Long--Short Portfolio Construction Architecture

## Objective

After the final investable signal has been constructed, the backtester
should support a direct **Pure Alpha long--short portfolio construction
path** that does not require a general numerical optimizer.

The Pure Alpha engine should be treated as a first-class
portfolio-construction method. Its output may:

-   be traded directly as a long--short strategy;
-   serve as a benchmark or research portfolio;
-   provide an alpha representation to a downstream long-only optimizer;
-   eventually be combined with other Pure Alphas in a higher-level
    alpha-combination layer.

The recommended direct construction is

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{s}_t^{\mathrm{final}}
&\xrightarrow{\text{Mapping}}
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t
\\
&\xrightarrow{\text{Exposure Projection}}
\mathbf w_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
\\
&\xrightarrow{\text{Pre-Isovol Scale}}
\mathbf w_t^{PA}
\\
&\xrightarrow{\text{Optional Isovol}}
\mathbf w_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

The stages are conceptually distinct even if a later implementation
combines some operations internally.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Final Investable Signal

Let

$$
\mathbf{s}_t^{\mathrm{final}}
=
(s_{1,t}^{\mathrm{final}},\ldots,s_{N_t,t}^{\mathrm{final}})^\top
\in\mathbb{R}^{N_t}
$$

denote the final investable signal at time (t).

This signal has already passed through the upstream signal-engine steps,
which may include:

-   raw characteristic construction;
-   outlier treatment;
-   score transformation;
-   signal neutralization;
-   multiple-signal combination;
-   smoothing;
-   confidence adjustments;
-   other signal-level transformations.

The Pure Alpha engine begins from this final signal and is responsible
for transforming it into portfolio positions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Signal-to-Weight Mapping

The first portfolio-construction step applies a configurable mapping
function

$$
g_t(\cdot)
$$

to the final signal:

$$
\boxed{
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t
=
g_t
\left(
\mathbf{s}_t^{\mathrm{final}}
\right).
}
$$

The resulting vector

$$
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t
\in\mathbb{R}^{N_t}
$$

should initially be interpreted as a **relative desired portfolio
direction**, rather than as the final portfolio.

Possible mappings include:

-   identity mapping;
-   rank-based mapping;
-   quantile mapping;
-   threshold mapping;
-   nonlinear mappings;
-   bounded mappings.

For example,

$$
g(s)=\tanh(\kappa s),
$$

where (`\kappa`{=tex}\>0) controls the aggressiveness of the
transformation.

The mapping stage should be modular so that different mapping functions
can be substituted without changing the downstream Pure Alpha
construction logic.

Importantly, portfolio neutrality should not be assumed merely because
the upstream signal was centered. A nonlinear mapping can destroy zero
cross-sectional mean.

Thus,

$$
\sum_i s_{i,t}^{\mathrm{final}}=0
$$

does not generally imply

$$
\sum_i g(s_{i,t}^{\mathrm{final}})=0.
$$

Portfolio exposure requirements should therefore be imposed explicitly
by the portfolio engine.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. General Exposure Projection

Rather than implementing separate mandatory procedures such as "dollar
neutralization" and "beta neutralization," the Pure Alpha engine should
use a general exposure-projection abstraction.

Let

$$
\mathbf A_t
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf a_{1,t} &
\cdots &
\mathbf a_{K,t}
\end{bmatrix}
\in
\mathbb{R}^{N_t\times K}
$$

denote the matrix of portfolio exposures to control.

Let

$$
\mathbf c_t
=
\begin{bmatrix}
c_{1,t}\\
\vdots\\
c_{K,t}
\end{bmatrix}
\in
\mathbb{R}^{K}
$$

denote the corresponding target exposures.

The desired portfolio must satisfy

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf A_t^\top\mathbf w_t
=
\mathbf c_t.
}
$$

The Pure Alpha engine should construct the portfolio closest to the
mapped direction (`\widetilde{\mathbf w}`{=tex}\_t) while satisfying
these exposure targets.

Under the Euclidean metric, solve

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
=
\arg\min_{\mathbf w}
\frac{1}{2}
\left\|
\mathbf w-\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t
\right\|_2^2
}
$$

subject to

$$
\mathbf A_t^\top\mathbf w
=
\mathbf c_t.
$$

The closed-form solution is

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
=
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t
-
\mathbf A_t
\left(
\mathbf A_t^\top\mathbf A_t
\right)^{-1}
\left(
\mathbf A_t^\top\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t
-
\mathbf c_t
\right).
}
$$

By construction,

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf A_t^\top
\mathbf w_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
=
\mathbf c_t.
}
$$

In production code, the implementation should use a stable linear solve
or pseudoinverse where appropriate rather than explicitly computing a
matrix inverse.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Default Pure Alpha Exposure Configuration

For the standard market-neutral Pure Alpha portfolio, define

$$
\mathbf A_t
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf 1 &
\boldsymbol{\beta}_t
\end{bmatrix},
$$

where

$$
\mathbf 1\in\mathbb{R}^{N_t}
$$

is the vector of ones and

$$
\boldsymbol{\beta}_t
=
(\beta_{1,t},\ldots,\beta_{N_t,t})^\top
$$

is the point-in-time vector of security market betas.

Set

$$
\mathbf c_t
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
0
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The resulting portfolio then satisfies

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf 1^\top
\mathbf w_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
=
0
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\boldsymbol{\beta}_t^\top
\mathbf w_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
=
0.
}
$$

The first condition imposes dollar neutrality.

The second imposes market-beta neutrality.

These are distinct portfolio properties and should be validated
independently.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Optional and Non-Zero Exposure Targets

The general exposure-projection framework should not hard-code dollar
neutrality.

### Non-Zero Net Exposure

If the desired net exposure is

$$
k\in\mathbb{R},
$$

use

$$
\mathbf c_t
=
\begin{bmatrix}
k\\
0
\end{bmatrix}
$$

for a portfolio with target net exposure (k) and target market beta
equal to zero.

This imposes

$$
\mathbf1^\top\mathbf w_t^{\mathrm{exp}}=k
$$

and

$$
\boldsymbol{\beta}_t^\top
\mathbf w_t^{\mathrm{exp}}=0.
$$

### Unconstrained Net Exposure

If net exposure should not be constrained, omit the intercept column:

$$
\mathbf A_t
=
\boldsymbol{\beta}_t,
\qquad
\mathbf c_t=0.
$$

Then only beta neutrality is imposed.

### General Exposure Targets

The same abstraction can later accommodate additional portfolio-level
exposure targets, for example:

-   industries;
-   sectors;
-   countries;
-   size;
-   volatility;
-   style factors.

Conceptually,

$$
\mathbf A_t
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf1 &
\boldsymbol\beta_t &
\mathbf B_t^{\mathrm{industry}} &
\mathbf B_t^{\mathrm{country}} &
\mathbf B_t^{\mathrm{style}}
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Only the exposures explicitly requested by the strategy configuration
should be included.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Homogeneous vs. Non-Homogeneous Exposure Targets

An important distinction must be made between zero and non-zero exposure
targets.

A homogeneous constraint has the form

$$
\mathbf A_t^\top\mathbf w_t=\mathbf0.
$$

If the portfolio is multiplied by any scalar (`\lambda`{=tex}),

$$
\mathbf A_t^\top
(\lambda\mathbf w_t)
=
\lambda
\mathbf A_t^\top\mathbf w_t
=
\mathbf0.
$$

Therefore, homogeneous exposure constraints are preserved by subsequent
scalar scaling.

Examples include:

-   zero net exposure;
-   zero beta exposure;
-   zero sector exposure;
-   zero style-factor exposure.

By contrast, for

$$
\mathbf A_t^\top\mathbf w_t=\mathbf c_t,
\qquad
\mathbf c_t\neq\mathbf0,
$$

scalar scaling gives

$$
\mathbf A_t^\top
(\lambda\mathbf w_t)
=
\lambda\mathbf c_t.
$$

The original target is therefore not preserved unless
(`\lambda=1`{=tex}).

This distinction is important for both gross-exposure normalization and
volatility targeting.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Pre-Isovol Portfolio Scale

Once the desired relative composition and homogeneous exposure
constraints have been established, the Pure Alpha portfolio may be
assigned a pre-isovol gross exposure.

Let

$$
G^{PA}>0
$$

denote the desired pre-isovol gross exposure.

For the standard zero-net, zero-beta Pure Alpha configuration, define

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^{PA}
=
G^{PA}
\frac{
\mathbf w_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
}{
\left\|
\mathbf w_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
\right\|_1
}.
}
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{
\left\|
\mathbf w_t^{PA}
\right\|_1
=
G^{PA}.
}
$$

Because this is a scalar transformation, zero exposure constraints
remain satisfied:

$$
\mathbf1^\top\mathbf w_t^{PA}=0,
$$

and

$$
\boldsymbol{\beta}_t^\top\mathbf w_t^{PA}=0.
$$

If the portfolio is dollar neutral, the long and short budgets are
automatically equal in absolute value:

$$
G_t^L
=
\frac{G^{PA}}{2},
$$

$$
G_t^S
=
-\frac{G^{PA}}{2}.
$$

For example, if

$$
G^{PA}=2,
$$

then

$$
\boxed{
G_t^L=+1,
\qquad
G_t^S=-1,
}
$$

corresponding to a (100/100) long--short portfolio.

### Non-Zero Exposure Targets

If an exact non-zero exposure such as

$$
\mathbf1^\top\mathbf w_t=k,
\qquad
k\neq0
$$

must be preserved, a subsequent scalar gross normalization generally
changes that target.

Therefore, for non-homogeneous exposure targets, scale and exposure
requirements must either:

1.  be solved jointly; or
2.  follow an explicitly defined priority rule.

The engine must not silently rescale a portfolio in a way that
invalidates an exact non-zero exposure target.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Pure Alpha Portfolio Invariants

Before optional volatility scaling, the engine should explicitly
validate the requested portfolio properties.

For the default zero-net, zero-beta Pure Alpha configuration:

$$
\boxed{
\left|
\mathbf1^\top
\mathbf w_t^{PA}
\right|
<
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{net}}.
}
$$

Similarly,

$$
\boxed{
\left|
\boldsymbol{\beta}_t^\top
\mathbf w_t^{PA}
\right|
<
\varepsilon_{\beta}.
}
$$

If pre-isovol gross exposure is fixed:

$$
\boxed{
\left|
\left\|
\mathbf w_t^{PA}
\right\|_1
-
G^{PA}
\right|
<
\varepsilon_G.
}
$$

These should be treated as numerical invariants and tested
programmatically.

The engine should never assume that a previous transformation has
preserved an exposure merely because it was intended to do so.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. Optional Pure Alpha Volatility Targeting

Volatility targeting should be treated as an optional portfolio-level
risk-scaling step.

Let

$$
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
\in
\mathbb{R}^{N_t\times N_t}
$$

denote the point-in-time forecast covariance matrix.

The ex-ante volatility of the Pure Alpha portfolio is

$$
\boxed{
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}
=
\sqrt{
(\mathbf w_t^{PA})^\top
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
\mathbf w_t^{PA}
}.
}
$$

Let

$$
\sigma_{PA}^{*}
$$

denote the desired volatility target.

The unconstrained volatility multiplier is

$$
\boxed{
\lambda_t^{PA}
=
\frac{
\sigma_{PA}^{*}
}{
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}
}.
}
$$

If leverage or other scaling limits apply, define an effective
multiplier such as

$$
\lambda_t
=
\min
\left(
\lambda_t^{PA},
\lambda_{\max},
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{feasible}}
\right).
$$

The risk-scaled Pure Alpha portfolio is

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}
=
\lambda_t
\mathbf w_t^{PA}.
}
$$

For homogeneous zero-exposure constraints, scalar isovol scaling
preserves neutrality:

$$
\mathbf1^\top
\mathbf w_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}
=
0,
$$

and

$$
\boldsymbol{\beta}_t^\top
\mathbf w_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}
=
0.
$$

Gross exposure becomes

$$
\left\|
\mathbf w_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}
\right\|_1
=
\lambda_t G^{PA}.
$$

This is intentional.

Pre-isovol gross exposure defines the structural scale of the Pure Alpha
construction, whereas isovol determines its final risk scale.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Recommended Pure Alpha Pipeline

The recommended architecture is therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{s}_t^{\mathrm{final}}
&\xrightarrow{\text{SignalWeightMapper}}
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t
\\
&\xrightarrow{\text{ExposureProjector}}
\mathbf w_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
\\
&\xrightarrow{\text{PortfolioNormalizer}}
\mathbf w_t^{PA}
\\
&\xrightarrow{\text{VolatilityScaler, optional}}
\mathbf w_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

In software terms:

``` text
FinalSignal
    |
    v
SignalWeightMapper
    |
    v
ExposureProjector
    |
    v
PortfolioNormalizer
    |
    v
PureAlphaPortfolio
    |
    v
VolatilityScaler [optional]
    |
    v
RiskScaledPureAlphaPortfolio
```

The Pure Alpha engine should remain independent from a general numerical
portfolio optimizer.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. Preserve Both Structural and Risk-Scaled Pure Alpha Weights

The engine should preserve at least two economically distinct outputs.

### Structural Pure Alpha Portfolio

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^{PA}
}
$$

This represents the portfolio after mapping, exposure control, and
pre-isovol normalization.

It captures the relative Pure Alpha portfolio construction before
portfolio-level volatility scaling.

### Risk-Scaled Pure Alpha Portfolio

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}
}
$$

This is the portfolio after optional volatility targeting and is the
natural candidate for direct trading when the Pure Alpha strategy itself
is deployed.

The distinction should not be collapsed merely because some
configurations set

$$
\lambda_t=1.
$$

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 12. Pure Alpha as an Input to a Long-Only Optimizer

A Pure Alpha portfolio may later feed a separate long-only
portfolio-construction engine.

However, the long-only optimizer should conceptually consume an **alpha
or expected-return representation**, not automatically the risk-scaled
Pure Alpha weights.

The risk-scaled portfolio

$$
\mathbf w_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}
$$

contains a portfolio-level risk-scaling decision through
(`\lambda`{=tex}\_t).

That scale does not necessarily imply stronger stock-level expected
returns.

Therefore, do not automatically define

$$
\widehat{\boldsymbol\mu}_t
=
\mathbf w_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}.
$$

Instead, the Pure Alpha engine should expose a separate alpha
representation, for example

$$
\boxed{
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_t^{PA}
\propto
\mathbf w_t^{PA},
}
$$

or a separately calibrated expected-return vector

$$
\boxed{
\widehat{\boldsymbol\mu}_t
=
h
\left(
\mathbf{s}_t^{\mathrm{final}},
\mathbf w_t^{PA},
\text{historical calibration}
\right).
}
$$

The long-only optimizer may then solve, for example,

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^{LO}
=
\arg\max_{\mathbf w\in\mathcal W_t^{LO}}
\left[
\mathbf w^\top
\widehat{\boldsymbol\mu}_t
-
\frac{\gamma}{2}
\mathbf w^\top
\widehat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_t
\mathbf w
-
C_t(\mathbf w)
\right].
}
$$

Here,

-   (`\widehat{\boldsymbol\mu}`{=tex}\_t) represents expected alpha;
-   (`\widehat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}`{=tex}\_t) represents risk;
-   (C_t(`\mathbf `{=tex}w)) represents implementation costs;
-   (`\mathcal `{=tex}W_t\^{LO}) represents the feasible long-only
    portfolio set.

This preserves the distinction between:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Alpha}
\neq
\text{Standalone Pure Alpha Risk Allocation}.
}
$$

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 13. Global Portfolio-Construction Architecture

After the final signal, the broader backtester may support two principal
portfolio-construction paths.

### Direct Pure Alpha Path

$$
\mathbf{s}_t^{\mathrm{final}}
\rightarrow
\mathbf w_t^{PA}
\rightarrow
\mathbf w_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}.
$$

This path constructs a transparent long--short portfolio directly.

### Optimization Path

$$
\mathbf{s}_t^{\mathrm{final}}
\rightarrow
\widehat{\boldsymbol\mu}_t
\rightarrow
\mathbf w_t^{\mathrm{target}}.
$$

This path uses the signal as an input to a numerical optimizer.

### Pure Alpha Followed by Optimization

The two paths may also be composed:

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf{s}_t^{\mathrm{final}}
\rightarrow
\text{Pure Alpha Engine}
\rightarrow
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_t^{PA}
\rightarrow
\text{Long-Only Optimizer}
\rightarrow
\mathbf w_t^{LO}.
}
$$

The Pure Alpha construction should therefore be treated as a reusable
portfolio/alpha representation rather than merely as an intermediate
implementation detail.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14. Recommended Software Components

A possible V1 decomposition is:

``` text
PureAlphaEngine
|
|-- SignalWeightMapper
|
|-- ExposureProjector
|
|-- PortfolioNormalizer
|
|-- VolatilityScaler [optional]
|
`-- PureAlphaDiagnostics
```

### `SignalWeightMapper`

Responsibility:

``` text
final_signal -> mapped_weights
```

It should not enforce portfolio-level exposure constraints unless a
specific mapping mathematically requires them.

### `ExposureProjector`

Responsibility:

``` text
mapped_weights
+ exposure_matrix
+ exposure_targets
-> exposure_adjusted_weights
```

It should support optional exposure constraints rather than hard-coding
dollar and beta neutrality.

### `PortfolioNormalizer`

Responsibility:

``` text
exposure_adjusted_weights
-> structural_pure_alpha_weights
```

For the standard homogeneous Pure Alpha case, this may normalize gross
exposure.

### `VolatilityScaler`

Responsibility:

``` text
pure_alpha_weights
+ point_in_time_risk_estimate
-> risk_scaled_weights
```

It should only determine portfolio scale and should not redefine
relative portfolio direction.

### `PureAlphaDiagnostics`

Responsibility:

-   calculate net exposure;
-   calculate gross exposure;
-   calculate long and short exposure;
-   calculate beta exposure;
-   calculate other configured factor exposures;
-   verify numerical invariants;
-   report forecast volatility;
-   report the volatility multiplier.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 15. Suggested Pure Alpha Result Object

The Pure Alpha engine should return enough intermediate state for
diagnostics, reproducibility, research, and downstream optimization.

Conceptually:

``` python
PureAlphaResult:
    final_signal
    mapped_weights
    exposure_adjusted_weights
    pure_alpha_weights
    risk_scaled_weights

    alpha_vector

    exposure_targets
    realized_exposures

    forecast_volatility
    volatility_target
    scale_factor

    diagnostics
```

Not every field must be implemented immediately in V1, but the
architecture should preserve the conceptual distinction between these
objects.

In particular:

``` text
alpha_vector
```

and

``` text
risk_scaled_weights
```

must not be treated as synonymous.

The alpha vector represents investment attractiveness or expected return
information.

The risk-scaled weights represent the amount of capital/risk assigned to
a standalone Pure Alpha strategy.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 16. V1 Methodological Choice

For V1, the recommended default is:

-   Euclidean signal-to-exposure projection;
-   zero net exposure;
-   zero market-beta exposure;
-   configurable gross exposure;
-   optional volatility targeting;
-   no general numerical optimizer inside the Pure Alpha engine.

Thus,

$$
\mathbf A_t
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf1 &
\boldsymbol\beta_t
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mathbf c_t
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
0
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The exposure-adjusted weights are

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
=
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t
-
\mathbf A_t
\left(
\mathbf A_t^\top\mathbf A_t
\right)^{-1}
\mathbf A_t^\top
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t.
}
$$

The structural Pure Alpha weights are

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^{PA}
=
G^{PA}
\frac{
\mathbf w_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
}{
\left\|
\mathbf w_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
\right\|_1
}.
}
$$

If volatility targeting is enabled,

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}
=
\lambda_t
\mathbf w_t^{PA},
}
$$

with

$$
\lambda_t
=
\frac{
\sigma_{PA}^{*}
}{
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}
}
$$

before application of any leverage or feasibility caps.

This gives a fully closed-form direct Pure Alpha construction for the
standard configuration.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 17. Future Extension: Weighted Exposure Projection

V1 should use the simple Euclidean projection.

A future extension may allow a configurable weighted metric:

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^*
=
\arg\min_{\mathbf w}
\frac12
(\mathbf w-\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t)^\top
\mathbf W_t
(\mathbf w-\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t)
}
$$

subject to

$$
\mathbf A_t^\top\mathbf w
=
\mathbf c_t.
$$

This provides a WLS-style portfolio projection in which deviations from
the original desired weights can be penalized differently across
securities.

Possible weighting schemes could eventually reflect:

-   liquidity;
-   market capitalization;
-   confidence;
-   transaction costs;
-   estimation precision;
-   other economically motivated metrics.

This functionality should not be implemented in V1 unless explicitly
required.

The V1 architecture should merely avoid making such an extension
impossible.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Final Design Principle

The Pure Alpha engine should answer:

> Given a final cross-sectional investment signal, what is the simplest
> transparent long--short portfolio that expresses that signal while
> satisfying explicitly requested portfolio exposures and risk scaling?

The recommended separation is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Signal Direction}
\rightarrow
\text{Exposure Control}
\rightarrow
\text{Structural Portfolio Scale}
\rightarrow
\text{Optional Risk Scale}.
}
$$

This keeps the direct Pure Alpha construction mathematically
transparent, computationally inexpensive, testable, and reusable both as
a standalone long--short strategy and as an upstream alpha input to more
complex portfolio optimizers.


# Pure Alpha Beta-Neutral Construction: Recommended Closed-Form Approach

## Decision

For the default Pure Alpha long--short construction, use a **joint
closed-form exposure projection** rather than a two-scalar
long-leg/short-leg rescaling.

The structural requirements of the standard Pure Alpha portfolio are:

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf 1^\top \mathbf w = 0
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\boldsymbol{\beta}^\top \mathbf w = 0.
}
$$

After these zero-exposure constraints have been imposed, restore the
desired gross exposure through a single scalar normalization.

This construction allows the final portfolio to satisfy, simultaneously
and up to numerical tolerance,

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf 1^\top \mathbf w^{PA}=0,
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\beta}^\top \mathbf w^{PA}=0,
\qquad
\|\mathbf w^{PA}\|_1=G.
}
$$

No iterative reconciliation or tolerated residual net exposure is
required for the standard zero-net, zero-beta case.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Why Two-Scalar Leg Rescaling Is Not the Default

Suppose an existing portfolio is separated into a long leg (L) and a
short leg (S), and the only allowed transformation is

$$
w_i^{BN}
=
\begin{cases}
a w_i, & i\in L,\\
b w_i, & i\in S.
\end{cases}
$$

There are only two free parameters:

$$
a,\qquad b.
$$

Therefore, in general, only two independent constraints can be imposed
exactly.

For example, one may impose:

$$
a\beta_L+b\beta_S=0
$$

for exact beta neutrality, and

$$
aG^L+b|G^S|=G
$$

for exact gross exposure.

However, this does not generally also guarantee

$$
\mathbf1^\top\mathbf w^{BN}=0.
$$

Thus the two-scalar construction solves

$$
\boxed{
\text{Beta Neutrality}
+
\text{Gross Exposure}
}
$$

but not generally

$$
\boxed{
\text{Dollar Neutrality}
+
\text{Beta Neutrality}
+
\text{Gross Exposure}.
}
$$

For a portfolio whose defining economic objective is a dollar-neutral,
beta-neutral Pure Alpha long--short portfolio, accepting a residual net
exposure is therefore not the preferred default.

The two-leg approach remains potentially useful when preserving
within-leg relative weights and long/short membership is itself a hard
requirement. That is a different portfolio-construction problem.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Recommended Joint Exposure Projection

Let

$$
\widetilde{\mathbf w}
\in\mathbb R^N
$$

denote the mapped portfolio direction obtained from the final signal.

Let

$$
\boldsymbol{\beta}
=
(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_N)^\top
\in\mathbb R^N
$$

denote the point-in-time security beta vector.

Define the exposure matrix

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf A
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf1 & \boldsymbol{\beta}
\end{bmatrix}
\in\mathbb R^{N\times2}.
}
$$

The desired Pure Alpha portfolio must satisfy

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf A^\top\mathbf w=\mathbf0.
}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\mathbf1^\top\mathbf w=0
$$

and

$$
\boldsymbol{\beta}^\top\mathbf w=0.
$$

We seek the portfolio closest to the original mapped direction in
Euclidean distance:

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w^{BN}
=
\arg\min_{\mathbf w}
\frac12
\|\mathbf w-\widetilde{\mathbf w}\|_2^2
}
$$

subject to

$$
\mathbf A^\top\mathbf w=\mathbf0.
$$

The closed-form solution is

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w^{BN}
=
\widetilde{\mathbf w}
-
\mathbf A
(\mathbf A^\top\mathbf A)^{-1}
\mathbf A^\top
\widetilde{\mathbf w}.
}
$$

Equivalently, defining the residual-maker or orthogonal-projection
matrix

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf M_A
=
\mathbf I
-
\mathbf A
(\mathbf A^\top\mathbf A)^{-1}
\mathbf A^\top,
}
$$

we have

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w^{BN}
=
\mathbf M_A\widetilde{\mathbf w}.
}
$$

By construction,

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf A^\top\mathbf w^{BN}=\mathbf0.
}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf1^\top\mathbf w^{BN}=0
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\boldsymbol{\beta}^\top\mathbf w^{BN}=0.
}
$$

In implementation, do not explicitly form a matrix inverse when a stable
linear solve or pseudoinverse is more appropriate.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Gross Exposure Restoration

The projection generally changes gross exposure:

$$
\|\mathbf w^{BN}\|_1
\neq G.
$$

This is not a problem.

Once dollar neutrality and beta neutrality have been imposed, restore
the desired pre-isovol gross exposure with a single scalar:

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w^{PA}
=
G
\frac{\mathbf w^{BN}}
{\|\mathbf w^{BN}\|_1}.
}
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{
\|\mathbf w^{PA}\|_1=G.
}
$$

Because dollar neutrality and beta neutrality are homogeneous zero
constraints, scalar multiplication preserves them.

Indeed,

$$
\mathbf1^\top\mathbf w^{PA}
=
\frac{G}{\|\mathbf w^{BN}\|_1}
\mathbf1^\top\mathbf w^{BN}
=
0,
$$

and

$$
\boldsymbol{\beta}^\top\mathbf w^{PA}
=
\frac{G}{\|\mathbf w^{BN}\|_1}
\boldsymbol{\beta}^\top\mathbf w^{BN}
=
0.
$$

Hence the final structural Pure Alpha portfolio satisfies all three
desired properties:

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf1^\top\mathbf w^{PA}=0,
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\boldsymbol{\beta}^\top\mathbf w^{PA}=0,
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\|\mathbf w^{PA}\|_1=G.
}
$$

For a dollar-neutral portfolio, the long and short gross exposures are
automatically symmetric:

$$
G^L=\frac{G}{2},
\qquad
G^S=-\frac{G}{2}.
$$

For example, if

$$
G=2,
$$

the resulting portfolio is (100/100):

$$
G^L=+1,
\qquad
G^S=-1.
$$

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Why This Construction Is Cleaner

The important property is that the neutrality constraints have zero
targets:

$$
\mathbf A^\top\mathbf w=\mathbf0.
$$

For any scalar (c),

$$
\mathbf A^\top(c\mathbf w)
=
c\mathbf A^\top\mathbf w
=
\mathbf0.
$$

Therefore, once the portfolio lies in the dollar-neutral and
beta-neutral subspace, subsequent scalar normalization cannot destroy
those neutralities.

This gives a clean separation between:

1.  **portfolio direction and exposure control**, and
2.  **portfolio scale**.

The exposure projection determines the admissible portfolio direction.

Gross normalization determines the desired pre-isovol scale.

Optional volatility targeting can subsequently determine the final risk
scale.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Recommended Pure Alpha Pipeline

The mathematically clean default pipeline is

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf s_t^{\mathrm{final}}
&\xrightarrow{\text{Mapping }g}
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t
\\
&\xrightarrow{\text{Joint Net + Beta Projection}}
\mathbf w_t^{BN}
\\
&\xrightarrow{\text{Gross Normalization}}
\mathbf w_t^{PA}
\\
&\xrightarrow{\text{Optional Isovol}}
\mathbf w_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

More explicitly:

### Stage A -- Mapping

$$
\boxed{
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t
=
g(\mathbf s_t^{\mathrm{final}}).
}
$$

### Stage B -- Joint Dollar- and Beta-Neutral Projection

Define

$$
\mathbf A_t
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf1 & \boldsymbol{\beta}_t
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^{BN}
=
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t
-
\mathbf A_t
(\mathbf A_t^\top\mathbf A_t)^{-1}
\mathbf A_t^\top
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t.
}
$$

### Stage C -- Gross Normalization

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^{PA}
=
G
\frac{\mathbf w_t^{BN}}
{\|\mathbf w_t^{BN}\|_1}.
}
$$

### Stage D -- Optional Isovol

If volatility targeting is enabled,

$$
\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}
=
\sqrt{
(\mathbf w_t^{PA})^\top
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_t
\mathbf w_t^{PA}
},
$$

$$
\lambda_t
=
\frac{\sigma_{PA}^*}{\widehat{\sigma}_{PA,t}},
$$

subject to any leverage or feasibility caps, and

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}
=
\lambda_t\mathbf w_t^{PA}.
}
$$

Because isovol is also scalar multiplication, zero net and zero beta
remain preserved.

## Relationship to the Six Inspectable Stages

The implementation may still expose the six previously named objects:

``` text
raw_mapped_weights
dollar_neutral_weights
exposure_normalized_weights
beta_neutral_weights
pure_alpha_weights
isovol_weights
```

However, these names should be interpreted as **inspectable pipeline
checkpoints**, not as a requirement that six independent transformations
must mechanically feed into one another.

In particular, the mathematically authoritative Pure Alpha construction
should not perform:

``` text
demean
-> independently normalize long/short legs
-> beta-adjust
-> tolerate resulting net drift
```

when the actual objective is exact zero net, exact zero beta, and fixed
gross exposure.

The joint exposure projection should be the authoritative mechanism used
to construct the final neutral portfolio direction.

Intermediate objects may still be calculated and retained for:

-   diagnostics;
-   debugging;
-   transparency;
-   research inspection;
-   comparison of alternative construction methods.

But they should not force the final construction into an unnecessarily
path-dependent sequence.

### `raw_mapped_weights`

$$
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t
=
g(\mathbf s_t^{\mathrm{final}}).
$$

This is the unconstrained portfolio direction implied by the signal
mapping.

An optional diagnostic showing what simple demeaning alone would

\mathbf w_t^{DN}
=
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t
-
\frac{1}{N_t}
\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t.

This object is useful for inspection but does not need to be the

### `exposure_normalized_weights`

An optional diagnostic showing the portfolio under a chosen gross or

Again, it need not define the final portfolio construction path.

### `beta_neutral_weights`

This should correspond to the authoritative joint zero-net, zero-beta
projection:

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^{BN}
=
\left[
\mathbf I
-
\mathbf A_t
(\mathbf A_t^\top\mathbf A_t)^{-1}
\mathbf A_t^\top
\right]
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t.
}
$$

Despite the historical name `beta_neutral_weights`, this object is
jointly dollar neutral and beta neutral when the intercept is included

A clearer long-term name may be:

``` text
exposure_projected_weights
```

or

``` text
neutralized_weights
```

while retaining compatibility aliases if necessary.

### `pure_alpha_weights`

This is the projected portfolio after restoring target gross exposure:

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^{PA}
=
G
\frac{\mathbf w_t^{BN}}
{\|\mathbf w_t^{BN}\|_1}.

### `isovol_weights`

This is the optional risk-scaled Pure Alpha portfolio:

\mathbf w_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}

## Required Numerical Invariants

For the standard Pure Alpha configuration, the engine should verify
after gross normalization:

$$
\boxed{
\left|
\mathbf1^\top\mathbf w_t^{PA}
\right|
<
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{net}},
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\left|
\boldsymbol{\beta}_t^\top\mathbf w_t^{PA}
\right|
<
\varepsilon_{\beta},
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\left|
\|\mathbf w_t^{PA}\|_1-G
\right|
<
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gross}}.
}
$$

Failure of one of these invariants should not be silently accepted as a
normal consequence of the construction.

Depending on the configured numerical policy, failure should trigger:

-   an explicit exception;
-   a failed validation result;
-   or a clearly surfaced warning.

The tolerance policy should itself be configurable and tested.

## Important Tradeoff: Sign and Membership Changes

The joint projection has one important economic consequence.

Because individual weights are adjusted in the exposure projection, it
is possible that a security initially mapped to a small long position
becomes short, or vice versa.

Thus the projection preserves the portfolio as closely as possible in
Euclidean distance while satisfying the neutrality constraints, but it
does **not** guarantee preservation of the original long/short
membership.

This is the fundamental tradeoff relative to two-leg scaling.

### Joint Projection

Advantages:

-   exact dollar neutrality;
-   exact beta neutrality;
-   gross exposure can subsequently be restored exactly;
-   closed-form solution;
-   no general numerical optimizer required;
-   naturally generalizes to additional linear exposure constraints.

Cost:

-   individual relative weights may change;
-   some positions may cross zero.

### Two-Leg Scaling

Advantages:

-   preserves within-leg relative weights;
-   preserves long/short membership when the leg multipliers remain
    positive;
-   computationally trivial.

Cost:

-   with only two leg multipliers, dollar neutrality, beta neutrality,
    and gross exposure cannot generally all be imposed independently.

For the default Pure Alpha engine, exact exposure neutrality is
considered more fundamental than preserving every original long/short
assignment. Therefore, the joint projection is the recommended default.

If preserving signs or membership later becomes a hard portfolio
constraint, that should be treated as a different constrained
portfolio-construction problem rather than silently approximated by the
default Pure Alpha engine.

## Generalization

The projection architecture naturally extends beyond market beta.

For example,

$$
\mathbf A_t
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf1 &
\boldsymbol{\beta}_t &
\mathbf B_t^{\mathrm{industry}} &
\mathbf B_t^{\mathrm{country}} &
\mathbf B_t^{\mathrm{style}}
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

For zero target exposures,

$$
\mathbf c_t=\mathbf0,
$$

and the same projection applies:

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf w_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
=
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t
-
\mathbf A_t
(\mathbf A_t^\top\mathbf A_t)^{-1}
\mathbf A_t^\top
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t.
}
$$

The standard Pure Alpha beta-neutral construction is therefore a special
case of a general portfolio exposure projector.

## V1 Implementation Decision

For V1:

1.  Use the final investable signal as the input.
2.  Apply the configured signal-to-weight mapping.
3.  Use a joint Euclidean exposure projection for the required zero
    portfolio exposures.
4.  For the default Pure Alpha strategy, include:
    -   intercept / net exposure;
    -   market beta.
5.  Restore the configured gross exposure through scalar normalization.
6.  Verify net, beta, and gross invariants explicitly.
7.  Apply optional isovol only after the structural Pure Alpha portfolio
    has been constructed.
8.  Preserve intermediate diagnostic objects where useful, but do not
    let the historical six-stage naming force an inferior mathematical
    construction.
9.  Do not use two-scalar long/short rescaling as the default
    beta-neutralization mechanism.
10. Do not accept residual net exposure as normal when exact dollar
    neutrality is part of the strategy definition.

The resulting core construction is:

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf{s}_t^{\mathrm{final}}
\rightarrow
\widetilde{\mathbf w}_t
\rightarrow
\mathbf w_t^{BN}
\rightarrow
\mathbf w_t^{PA}
\rightarrow
\mathbf w_t^{PA,\mathrm{iso}}.
}
$$

with

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf1^\top\mathbf w_t^{PA}=0,
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\beta}_t^\top\mathbf w_t^{PA}=0,
\qquad
\|\mathbf w_t^{PA}\|_1=G.
}
$$

This is the recommended closed-form default for the direct Pure Alpha
long--short engine.
